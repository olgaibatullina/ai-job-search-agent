Update (2026-11-03): Further investigation identified repetitive reasoning loops in Qwen3 8B as a known failure mode reported by other researchers. Our LangSmith traces independently confirmed this behavior: the judge repeatedly generated the same reasoning until exhausting its 4,096-token output limit without producing valid JSON.

Update (2026-11-03): Disabling thinking reduced failures (7/75 → 1/75), latency (−57.7%), and output tokens (−64.6%), with nearly unchanged mean Faithfulness scores.
However, q010 failed 10/10 repeated runs during Verdict Generation. Ollama aborted generation due to token repeat limit reached, resulting in InstructorRetryException.

Regression: Thinking OFF improves reliability but does not eliminate repetition loops. Judge accuracy remains unverified.

Next steps: H7 — sampling optimization and repetition mitigation.
Hypothesis#7Optimizing sampling parameters and introducing repetition controls can reduce Qwen3 reasoning loops while preserving thinking mode and evaluation accuracy.Test sampling parameters (temperature, top_p, top_k), repetition penalties, and a fallback retry strategy. Change one parameter at a time.Reduced failure rate, token consumption, and latency without degrading judge accuracy.Partially confirmedThe final configuration (temperature=0.6, top_p=0.95, repeat_penalty=1.1) reduced evaluation failures from 7/75 to 0/75 and average output tokens from 2,693 to 2,492. On the 68 commonly successful cases, mean Faithfulness changed only slightly (0.8696 → 0.8645).

Regression: Manual inspection revealed inconsistent verdict behavior, including potential false positives. Execution reliability improved, but judge accuracy preservation remains unverified without human-labeled ground truth.
